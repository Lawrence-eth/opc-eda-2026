I am writing to ask about how to handle a potential constraint conflict in some test cases.  In particular, I found that in some examples, two modules may satisfy the following conditions at the same time:  They belong to the same boundary constraint. They are both preplaced modules. One of the preplaced modules does not touch the corresponding boundary. In this case, if we strictly enforce both the preplaced-position constraint and the 

boundary constraint, the problem appears to become infeasible. Could you please clarify how such cases are expected to be handled during evaluation?  Should we:  Prioritize keeping the preplaced modules fixed and allow the boundary constraint to be partially violated; Prioritize satisfying the boundary constraint and adjust the preplaced module accordingly; Treat the case as infeasible; Or follow some other specified rule? I would appreciate your clarification on the expected handling of such conflicting constraints so that we can make our implementation consistent with the evaluation rules.  A5. In the contest evaluator, preplaced position is a hard constraint, and boundary touching is a soft constraint. They operate at different levels and there is no ambiguity about priority. Preplaced position (hard constraint): A preplaced block must appear at exactly the (x, y, w, h) specified in the input. Any deviation makes the solution infeasible and assigns Cost = M = 10 for that test case. This cannot be overridden by any soft constraint. Boundary touching (soft constraint): Whether a block touches its required boundary edge or corner is checked as part of V_soft. A violation increments the boundary violation count, which raises the exponential penalty term exp(β · V_relative). The solution remains feasible. The correct handling: Keep all preplaced modules at their fixed positions. If a preplaced module's fixed position does not touch its boundary constraint, that boundary violation is counted in V_soft and penalized through the cost function, but the solution is still feasible. You should never move a preplaced module to satisfy a soft constraint.   Q6. For the soft blocks, can they exceed their area target?  If the area target is 100, can the block be 12x12?  The reason I ask is that in order to get abutment between a pair of blocks, or to have a block touch the side or top of the placement area, it might be useful to stretch the block in one or more directions.  

The contest specifies a 1% area tolerance, and going below the target area is clearly a violation, but it seems reasonable to allow things to be larger.  A6. No. The 1% area tolerance is enforced symmetrically in both directions using an absolute relative error check: |w × h − area_target| / area_target ≤ 0.01 Both exceeding and falling short of the target by more than 1% count as a hard constraint violation, making the solution infeasible (Cost = M = 10). A block with target area 100 placed as 12×12 (area = 144, +44% excess) would be infeasible. The rationale is that soft blocks have a target area reflecting the actual silicon area required by the module. Allowing unconstrained growth would let a solver trivially satisfy grouping, MIB, and boundary soft constraints by inflating block sizes, circumventing the intent of those constraints. The 1% tolerance exists only to accommodate minor aspect-ratio adjustments while preserving the overall die area budget. If you need a block to touch a boundary or abut a neighbor, the correct approach is to adjust its aspect ratio (width/height) while keeping w × h within 1% of the target area, not to increase the area itself.
